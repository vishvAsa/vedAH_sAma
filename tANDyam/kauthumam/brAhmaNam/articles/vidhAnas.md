Notes on vidhāna literature and sāmavidhāna brāhmaṇa:

In earlier threads, we briefly saw a conspectus and the evolution of gṛhya rituals. A natural evolution of that would be a discussion of rituals in the pariśiṣṭa literature followed by those in vidhāna literature. 

Rituals in gṛhya (and dharma) texts already show a variety of applications, going beyond saṃskāras (sacraments) and overlap with ātharvanik practices.+++(4)+++ We can see an inflation of the materials along the same themes across pariśiṣṭas and vidhānas. 

## Pariśiṣṭas
Pariśiṣṭas in shrauta context are appendices intended to clarify, capture and document ritual points that are not explicitly stated in brahmana and śrautasūtras. In an extreme case yajurvedic śrautasūtras have a hautra pariśiṣṭa, technically allowing an iṣṭi performer to ignore a Rigvedic hotṛ and make do with yajurvedic experts.+++(5)+++ 

## Gṛhya pariśiṣṭas
Gṛhya pariśiṣṭas, OTOH display the expected inflation of use cases - sandhyopāsana, ācamana, devapūja, grahapūja etc. to prāyaścittas and even installation of water tanks. 

## Vidhānas
Vidhānas can be thought of as texts composed to satisfy various desires with the express goal of using an alternative to shrauta ritual format. Technically, the almost all of them could be achieved by atharvavedic rituals. 

We can only guess the reason as "non-availability of atharvan experts" and reuse of Rig/Yajur/Saman materials for which experts are already available. 

śaunaka in ṛgvidhāna talks about 100s of desires - getting offspring, cows, wealth, getting rid of diseases and sin, confounding and destroying enemies, charming someone (usual atharvanic themes).

## sāmavidhāna
In this context, sāmavidhāna is special. It alone is counted as part of the brahmana literature. 

Moreover, it alone gives a series of sāmans whose application is intended to give the same results as establishing fires (agnyādhāna), performance of fortnightly iṣṭis, caturmāsyas, paśubandha and sautrāmaṇi. It then moves on to various soma sacrifice vidhāna substitutes.

I feel sāmavidhāna alone covering these use cases is not by accident. āpastamba is pretty blunt - he says agnihotra and its modifications only use Yajurveda; Iṣṭis need ṛgveda and Yajurveda; Soma saṃsthas need all the three vedas. 

### iṣṭis
So a Chandoga has no role in Iṣṭis (technically his presence is there in agnyādhāna but even that has been made optional by certain yajurvedic texts). Samavedins would have felt an acute need to have an equivalent of these agnihotra & iṣṭi rituals. 

### soma saṃsthas
They fulfilled this with the vidhāna equivalents. Soma rites typically are costly affairs and not everyone is going to be an āhitāgni - a fact recognized by gṛhyasūtras themselves. So it was natural for sāmavidhāna to cover soma saṃsthas as well. 

### adhikAra
An important point to note is that, just because the actual vidhāna ritual is much simpler, doesn't mean it's simple overall. The eligibility for these rites have been toughened up with multiple rounds of fasting, kṛcchras, lengthy japa of sāvitrī and vyāhṛtis, ritual baths, continuous japa of certain texts, tarpaṇas and then a sthālīpāka to certain devatas indicated. 

Yajurvidhāna even requires you to do this tapasya at a river bank, constructing a kusha hut, wearing kusha (even sacred thread is supposed to be from kusha fibers), take food only via bhikshā while completing the preliminaries. Only after this, your vidhāna rituals are expected to work.
